# Arm 2 of the ear test: clips produced by code, not by a model

Date: 2026-09-02. Six 60-second clips, three per palette, rendered by
`synth/render.py` in this repo. **No listening judgement appears anywhere in
this file** — the agent that wrote it cannot hear. Every number below is a
measurement from `synth/measure.py`, which is a port of the script that produced
`spec-from-references.md` (same librosa calls, same `n_fft=8192`, same band
edges) plus EBU R128 loudness from `ffmpeg`. The ear test is still the real test.

Audio lives outside the repo, in
`~/Documents/AI Agent Outputs/Artifacts/flow-audio-candidates/`, and is never
committed. No reference audio was used as input to anything here — the renderer
takes a seed, a palette and a BPM, and nothing else.

## What the renderer actually does, in plain English

It is a small synthesiser and a set of arrangement rules, about 400 lines of
numpy. There is no model and no sample library: every sound is built from
oscillators and filters at render time.

**The sounds.** The kick is a 909-style synthesis — a sine whose pitch falls
from about 158 Hz to 55 Hz in thirty milliseconds (the thump), plus damped
resonances at 138, 196 and 254 Hz (the body — this is the part that makes a kick
read as *fat* rather than just loud), plus a click, the whole thing driven
through soft saturation. The sub bass is a single sine at the root note, locked
to the kick by a sidechain duck, and pushed through tanh saturation so its second
and third harmonics land at 110–210 Hz; that is what carries a 55 Hz note on a
phone speaker. Above that sits a chord bed: one minor triad, three detuned saws
plus a layer of slowly-beating pure sines, through a resonant low-pass filter
whose cutoff drifts across 500 Hz over the length of the clip. Offbeat stabs of
the same chord feed a tempo-synced dub delay — dotted eighth, damped feedback,
tape-style wow on each successive repeat, ping-ponged left/right. Hats are
filtered noise, kept deliberately tiny. Palette B adds a two-to-three note motif
pushed hard into the delay so the echoes make the rhythm, a low-passed rave chord
stab every eight bars sitting far back in the reverb, and a distant muted
horn-like pad (FM, slow attack, heavily filtered) every twelfth bar.

**The rules.** Constant pulse, one key, no chord changes, and the only pitch
event in an entire palette-B clip is the sub lifting a minor third on the last
beat of every fourth bar. Nothing rises, drops, breaks, fills, or fades. The only
movement is slow and hidden: three modulators (chord cutoff, bed cutoff,
percussion level, echo colour) each on their own period and their own phase, so
they never line up into something that reads as a "section".

**Loop safety.** Clip length is a whole number of bars. Every modulator's period
divides that length. The renderer computes two full cycles and keeps the second,
so the echo and reverb tails from the previous cycle are already present at
sample zero — the clip butt-splices with no click and no fade. Measured: the
sample-to-sample step across the splice is smaller than the largest step that
already occurs inside the clip on all six clips (row "loop seam" below, all ≤ 0).

**How the balance is set.** Band power adds across stems, so each band's share of
the total is a linear function of the squared stem gains. That makes "hit the
reference band balance" a small bounded least-squares problem rather than a
guess: the renderer measures each stem's six-band energy, solves for the gains
that land the mix on ref01's distribution, and clamps them so the solver can
shape the spectrum but can never mute an element. Every clip is then mastered
through a glue bus compressor, soft clip and lookahead limiter.

**Determinism.** Verified, not assumed: re-rendering `--palette b --seed 41
--bpm 115` produced a byte-identical WAV (sha256 `155e448f…`) and byte-identical
stems.

## Outputs per clip

- `synth-<palette>-<seed>.wav` — the listening copy: −14 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP,
  full master chain (glue compressor → soft clip → limiter).
- `synth-<palette>-<seed>-unmastered-16lufs.wav` — the same mix at −16 LUFS with
  no glue compression and no saturation, peak-limited only.
- `stems/<name>/<name>-{kick,sub,chords,motif,fx}.wav` — five stems at the
  −16 LUFS mix level, for a later layering engine, plus a `-meta.json` recording
  the seed, key, tempo, solved gains and measured loudness.

### How this compares with the ElevenLabs arm

The six generated clips in `spec-from-references.md` put 87–97 % of their energy
below 150 Hz and only 3.1–12.0 % in the 150–500 Hz body region. These six put
46–50 % below 150 Hz and 46.5–49.3 % in the body — that is ref01's distribution

generated clips faded despite the prompt forbidding it; none of these do, because
a fade is not something the renderer can produce.

## What did not land inside the envelope, and why

**Slow timbral drift is about a third short.** Both references show a
1-second-smoothed spectral centroid with a coefficient of variation of 0.41–0.46.
These clips sit at 0.29–0.34 — the colour moves, but less than the references'
does. The cause is structural: the references drift because a human (or, for
ref02, a stem-assembly engine) keeps swapping and re-voicing material over
minutes, whereas here the drift is three sine LFOs on filter cutoffs. Pushing the
LFOs wider was tried and trades directly against the band balance — every extra
decibel of cutoff sweep moves energy across the 500 Hz boundary and pulls the
150–500 Hz share off target. This is the honest limit of a single-cycle renderer;
the fix is a longer-form layering engine over the exported stems, not a deeper
LFO.

**Frame-level RMS spread is lower than the references (5.2–7.3 dB vs 10–11 dB).**
These clips are *more* constant than the references, not less. LRA says the same
thing. Whether "more constant than the reference" is good or bad is a listening
question, not a measurement one.

**Sub band is right at the ceiling of the guidance.** The spec suggested the
sub band be no more than about half of the total below 150 Hz; these clips sit at
52–53 % of it. It is a fraction over, and it is over deliberately: pulling the
kick's fundamental down further thinned the low end audibly in the band table.

**Two clips read one tempo step low.** a-23 was rendered at 116.0 BPM and b-41 at
115.0, and librosa reports 114.8 for both. The grid is exact by construction (the
renderer places every event from the BPM), so this is the tempogram's resolution,
not a drift — beat-interval CV is 0.005–0.011 across all six, steadier than either
reference.

**500 Hz–2 kHz varies more than intended** (2.4–5.5 % against ref01's 4.2 %). It
is the band the least-squares balancer sacrifices when it is holding the three
that matter, and no clip is far from the references on it.

## How to run it

One command, from the repo root. It builds its own venv with `uv`, renders all
six clips with their stems, and writes the measurement table:

```
./synth/run.sh ~/Documents/AI\ Agent\ Outputs/Artifacts/flow-audio-candidates
```

A single clip:

```
synth/.venv/bin/python synth/render.py --palette b --seed 41 --bpm 117 --out ./out
```

The mastering chain is a standalone function so it can be applied to clips from
any other arm of the ear test:

```python
from master import master_chain
out, info = master_chain(audio, sr, target_lufs=-14.0, ceiling_dbtp=-1.0)
```

or from the shell on any WAV:

```
synth/.venv/bin/python synth/master.py in.wav out.wav --lufs -14 --ceiling -1
```

Rendering all six clips takes about six minutes on the Mac mini (M4). Everything
above was rendered and measured there; the WAVs were copied back to the laptop.

# Variants of a-11 after the first listen (2026-09-02)

Daniel listened to `synth-a-11.wav` on headphones — "not bad at all" — and asked
for three escalating variants changing three things: **less** of the echoey,
paddy chord layer; **thicker** bass; **more** of the hypnotic motif through the
tempo-synced delay. Same seed (11), same grid, same key (A, root 55 Hz), same
tempo (114.5 BPM set / 114.8 measured), same synthesis. Only balance and
arrangement move.

Variants are a table of parameters in `synth/render.py` (`VARIANT_SPECS`),
selected with `--variant v1|v2|v3`. Passing no variant reproduces the original
clips byte for byte — verified by re-rendering `synth-a-11.wav` after the
refactor and comparing sha256.

Three things are new synthesis rather than new levels. **The motif exists in
palette A for the first time** — the base palette-A clips have no motif at all,
so "more motif" meant adding the palette-B motif generator (same 2–3 notes, one
key, no runs) and letting the delay carry it. **Kick and sub are glued** through
one shared saturator: the summed bass is driven through tanh and the resulting
gain curve applied back to each part, which is what a bus saturator does — the
two stop fighting for the same peak and read as one instrument. Because the same
curve hits both, the stems still sum exactly. **Two extra bass layers** sit above
the fundamental: one an octave up (110 Hz, saturated, low-passed) and one at
three times the root (165 Hz), which is where a bass reads as fat on headphones.

The band-balance solver is held on a short leash for variants — tight bounds on
pad, motif and fx (0.80–1.25) so it cannot undo the arrangement, looser on kick
and sub (0.65–1.80) where its only job is keeping the band balance honest.

## Tradeoffs hit

**"Fat" and "share of energy below 150 Hz" pull in opposite directions.** The
bass is thicker in every sense that was asked for — saturated harmonics, an
octave-up layer, a 165 Hz body layer, longer kick body, bus glue — and yet the
*share* below 150 Hz went **down**, from 49.4 % on the base clip to 41–45 %.
That is not a contradiction, it is what "fat" means: the 80–250 Hz body that
makes a bass read on headphones sits mostly in the 150–500 Hz band, so adding it
moves the balance up the spectrum. The first attempt made this mistake in the
other direction — an octave-up layer alone piled energy into 60–150 Hz and pushed
v2's body region down to 42.9 %, below the floor, which is why the 165 Hz layer
exists.

**All three variants are now above ref01 on the body region** (50.9–54.0 % against
46.5 %). Taking the pad out and putting the motif in did not empty that band, it
re-voiced it — the motif and the bass body layers live in the same 150–500 Hz
region the pad used to occupy. If the goal becomes matching ref01 exactly rather
than following Daniel's arrangement note, the pad would have to come back.

**The clips got darker.** Spectral centroid falls from 1572 Hz on the base clip
to ~1030–1090 Hz on the variants, past ref01 (1797 Hz) and toward ref02 (788 Hz).
The pad's filter sweep was the brightest moving thing in the mix, and it is the
thing that was turned down.

**Slow timbral drift got worse before it got better.** Centroid slow CV drops to
0.24 on v1 — the pad's cutoff LFO was carrying most of the drift — then recovers
to 0.30 on v3 as the longer delay feedback and the sparse horn take over. All
three are still under the references' 0.41–0.46, for the same structural reason
given above.

**Frame-level RMS spread halved** (5.7 dB → 2.5–3.7 dB). These are now extremely
constant, further from the references' 10–11 dB than the base clip was. Whether
that is hypnotic or inert is a listening question.

# Round 5: loop-pack ingest and layering engine

**Synthesis is over.** Daniel rejected round 4 — all eight bass candidates "all
the same, computer-generated" — and measurement agreed: they sat within about
1 dB of each other in every band, because band-limiting, drive and loudness
normalisation applied in series erase whatever made a source distinctive. The
verdict is logged in `generation-log-2026-09-02.md`. Route A is now the plan:
professionally produced loops, used unprocessed, with the code allowed only to
**select, layer, crossfade and trim gain**.

This entry covers the engine, built and proven end-to-end on a throwaway fixture
while the real pack is being bought. Rounds 1–4 are in `surge-arm-2026-09-02.md`.

## What was built

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `synth/packs.py` | Ingest: scan a pack, parse BPM and key from filenames, categorise, measure, write the inventory markdown. **Never writes audio.** |
| `synth/layer.py` | `audition` (hear the library) and `combos` (layer it). The only filter in the file is `mono_below(120 Hz)` on the mix bus. |
| `synth/make_test_pack.py` | Throwaway fixture so the code could be exercised before the pack arrives. **Explicitly not audition material.** |
| `synth/run-packs.sh` | One command, same venv convention as `run.sh`: ingest → audition → combos. |

```
./synth/run-packs.sh <PACK_DIR> <PACK_NAME> [OUTDIR]
```

### Ingest

Filename parsing covers the Riemann convention and the common variants:
`..._124_Am.wav`, `124bpm`, `bpm124`, `A min`, `Amin`, `Aminor`, `F#m`, `Cmaj`.
A bare letter with no mode marker is read as minor, which is what these packs
mean by `Am`. Fourteen categories are matched against folder *and* filename by
**prefix**, in priority order — a whole-word match missed "Atmosphere",
"Percussion" and "Hihats", and prefix order is what keeps a "bassdrum" landing in
kick rather than bass.

Per file it records duration, **bars at the parsed BPM with a non-integer flag**,
EBU R128 integrated loudness, peak, stereo width (side/mid energy), and band
shares at the brief's edges (<60, 60–150, 150–600, 600–2k, >2k Hz). Files that
are not a whole number of bars are listed separately and skipped by the engine.
Output is `research/ear-test/pack-inventory-<pack>.md`.

### Audition — gain only, and that is the whole point

Bass: the six with the highest share below 150 Hz. Kick: the four with the
lowest peak frequency under 250 Hz. Chords and atmospheres: four each. Every clip
is the loop tiled to 16 s, then **a gain trim to −16 LUFS and a backoff if the
true peak would exceed −1 dBTP — nothing else**. No EQ, no drive, no
band-limiting, no reverb, and deliberately no limiter: if holding the ceiling
costs loudness, the clip is quieter and the shortfall is reported rather than
squashed. Output is wav + m4a (`-c:a aac -b:a 160k`) with `manifest.json` in the
player's `n` / `label` / `source` format, where `source` is the original filename
and `label` is a one-line description derived from the measurements.

### Layering engine

Target BPM and key are the pack's dominant values. **Decision, 2026-09-02: "no
processing" wins.** A loop joins a combo only if its BPM already matches the
target exactly and its key already matches exactly; drums with no key always
qualify. There is no transposition and no time-stretch of any size.

The contradiction that forced the decision: resampling shifts pitch and tempo
together, so the brief's ±1 semitone of transposition would be a 5.95 % tempo
change against a 1.5 % stretch budget — the two constraints could not both hold.
Both are now zero. `load()` refuses a transpose and refuses a BPM mismatch, with
the reason in the exception. The single resample still permitted is plain
**sample-rate conversion** (a 48 kHz file into a 44.1 kHz engine), which changes
neither tempo nor pitch and is a format change rather than processing.

**Gaps are reported, not filled.** Because nothing is bent to fit, a category
with no same-key, same-BPM, whole-bar candidates is a fact about the pack. The
combos step prints and records a per-category gap report — total files, usable
files, and how many were rejected for wrong BPM, wrong key or a ragged bar count
— into `manifest.json`, and a missing kick or bass fails loudly rather than
quietly substituting something transposed.

Kick and bass are anchors and run the whole clip. Chord (8 bars), atmos (12),
hat (12), perc (20) and top (20) enter and leave on bar boundaries with
equal-power crossfades — two bars for chord and atmos, one for the rest. Layers
are summed with headroom, `mono_below(120 Hz)` is applied to the bus, and the
same gain-only master finishes it. Sources are never re-EQ'd.
