## What each voice is now

| Stem | Before (numpy) | Now |
|---|---|---|
| kick | hand-written sine with pitch envelope + resonances | **Faust `sy.kick`, compiled at run time by DawDreamer** — no plugin at all. Twelve variants per clip with jittered pitch, decay, drive and click, plus a per-hit velocity drift |
| sub | sine + tanh + two added layers | **Surge XT**, one patch, three oscillators: sine at the root, sine an octave up, sine a fifth above that, through a 24 dB ladder low-pass |
| chords (pad) | summed band-limited saws | **Surge XT**, three unison saw oscillators, detuned, low-passed, held as one sustained triad |
| chords (stabs) | same, plucked envelope | **Surge XT**, same voicing with a 260 ms decay |
| motif | summed saw + sine | **Surge XT**, detuned saw + sine through a resonant low-pass |
| fx (delay) | hand-written expanded feedback delay | **Surge XT Effects, `fx_type = "Delay"`** — tempo-synced left/right times, damped feedback with its own low- and high-cut, crossfeed for ping-pong, and its own modulation for tape wow |
| fx (reverb) | convolution with a noise IR | pedalboard's `Reverb` (see the gotcha below) |
| saturation | tanh | pedalboard `Distortion` on the bass bus, plus the existing shared kick+sub glue |
| percussion | filtered noise bursts | **unchanged** — hats are noise, and no plugin improves that |

Producer techniques from §5 of the instrument study are all applied: saturation on
the kick+bass bus, per-hit drift on the kick (§5.2 — the argument for synthesis
over samples), sidechain-style ducking of the bass under the kick, and the
150–500 Hz band watched as the headline number (§5.3).

## The arrangement fix: one phrase, forever

Daniel on the previous numpy variants: *"all three … like a drunk child pressing
buttons"*, while the base a-11 with no motif at all was *"not bad at all"*. So the
motif generator was the problem, not the voices.

Reading the old code, it was not random — but it was **gated**: notes fired only
on even bars, and the third note only on every fourth bar, at beats 0.0, 1.75 and
2.5. Run that through a dotted-eighth echo with 58–68 % feedback and the result is
a shifting cloud with no repeating figure to latch onto. Measurably it was also a
loop defect: a two-bar gate does not tile a 29-bar clip, so the pattern's phase
jumped at the splice.

It is now **one fixed phrase, identical in every bar of the clip**: three notes at
beats **0.0, 1.5 and 2.5**, pitches **root, fifth, minor third**, two octaves above
the sub, no gates, no choices, no variation. A one-bar phrase tiles any bar count,
so the loop point is clean. The only thing that moves is a very slow filter opening
on the motif, one cycle per clip. Motif level is held at the v1 setting (0.85).
This is the Plastikman/Basic Channel rule: one hook, forever.

The same fix is applied to palette B, whose motif used the same gate.

## Gotchas found by running it

**Parameter names on Surge XT Effects are fixed at load time.** After
`fx.fx_type = "Reverb 2"` the exposed names are still `delay_time_left`,
`feedback_eq_feedback` and so on — the names belong to whichever effect was
selected when pedalboard loaded the plugin, while the meanings behind them change.
Each effect therefore gets its own freshly loaded instance, and reverb and
saturation use pedalboard's own well-named effects rather than guessing at aliased
slots. The study did not flag this one.

**Continuous parameters are quantised lists of formatted strings.** Assigning
`a_amp_eg_decay = "1000.0 ms"` raises, because the accepted list jumps
`996.5 ms → 1.01 s`. Every continuous parameter now goes through a `set_near()`
helper that parses the unit off each option and picks the closest.

**Every patch is proved by its audio.** Following the study's warning that a
parameter belonging to a disabled module reads back as set while doing nothing,
each patch asserts a non-silent render, and the three filtered patches additionally
render twice at different cutoffs and assert the audio actually differs. The audit
log is written into each clip's `-meta.json`. All assertions passed: bass, pad and
motif cutoffs changed the audio by 0.51, 0.68 and 0.59 peak amplitude respectively,
and all twelve Faust kicks came back audible.

## Tradeoffs and what still measures as synthetic

**The Surge clips are much brighter at 500 Hz–2 kHz: 7.2–8.3 % against the numpy
engine's 2.4–4.6 % and ref01's 4.2 %.** This is the clearest single difference
between the two engines and it is inherent: the numpy saws were built by summing
harmonics up to a hard 2.2–5 kHz ceiling, whereas Surge's Classic oscillator is
full-bandwidth and its ladder filter has a 24 dB/octave slope, not a brick wall.
Filter cutoffs were already pulled down (pad 520 → 430 Hz, stab 700 → 560 Hz,
motif 900 → 760 Hz) to get from 8.4 % to this, and pulling further starts eating
the 150–500 Hz body. **This is the one measurement where the numpy arm is closer
to the references than the real synth is.**

**Spectral centroid, on the other hand, got much closer to ref01.** 1683–1899 Hz
against ref01's 1797 Hz, where the numpy clips sat at 1028–1572 Hz. Two ways of
saying the same thing about the same spectra — which of the two matters is a
listening question, not a measurement one.

**Onset density fell to 329–364/min** (numpy 388–406, ref01 393, ref02 346). Surge's
amplitude envelopes have real attack curves rather than the numpy engine's
instantaneous hits, so the onset detector finds fewer discrete events. Still inside
the range the two references span.

**Slow timbral drift did not improve** (0.22–0.32 against the references' 0.41–0.46).
This is the same structural limit reported for the numpy arm: the drift here is a
handful of slow LFOs on filter cutoffs, while the references drift because material
is swapped and re-voiced over minutes. A real synth does not fix that; a
longer-form layering engine over the stems would.

**A regression in the numpy v1, caused by the motif fix.** The fixed phrase plays
three notes every bar where the gated one averaged about 1.5, so it adds
150–500 Hz energy: numpy v1's Σ <150 Hz fell from 42.5 % to **38.4 %, just under
the 40 % envelope floor**, and its body region rose to 57.0 %. The Surge v1 does
not have this problem (47.2 % / 45.4 %) because Surge's three-oscillator bass patch
through a 24 dB ladder carries considerably more energy below 150 Hz than the numpy
sub did. If the numpy variants matter going forward, their bass needs re-weighting;
if the Surge engine is the path, they do not.

**Loop seam on surge-a-11 is +0.9 dB**, i.e. the step across the splice is about
10 % larger than the largest step already inside the clip. In a clip whose only
transient is a kick and whose loop point lands exactly on one, that ratio is
essentially 1 by construction; the other two clips are −4.4 and −4.9 dB. Fixing the
kick's drift ladder to repeat per cycle (it previously ran a 12-long round robin
across a 116-beat cycle, so the two rendered cycles used different kicks) moved this
from a real defect to a rounding question.

**What still measures as synthetic, honestly.** Nothing in these numbers can say
whether the clips sound like a machine — but two things are measurably unlike both
references. Loudness range is **0.5–0.6 LU** against the references' 4.1 LU: these
are far flatter than real records, by design, and flatter than the numpy clips.
And frame-level RMS spread (6.9–9.2 dB) is still under the references' 10–11 dB.
A real 909 through a real desk would not hold a 0.5 LU range for a minute.

## How to run it

The numpy engine remains the default, and the six original clips still reproduce
byte for byte — verified after every refactor in this round by re-rendering
`synth-a-11` and `synth-b-41` and comparing sha256.

```
# Surge XT + Faust voices
synth/.venv/bin/python synth/render.py --palette a --seed 11 --bpm 114.5 \
    --engine surge --name surge-a-11 --out <outdir>

# the original numpy oscillators (unchanged, byte-identical)
synth/.venv/bin/python synth/render.py --palette a --seed 11 --bpm 114.5 --out <outdir>
```

overridable with `FLOW_SURGE_DIR`. Requires `pedalboard`, `mido` and, for the Faust
kick, `dawdreamer`.

## Rule-by-rule

| # | Rule | Status | What was done |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Kick 1/5/9/13, closed hat 3/7/11/15, **one** clap on step 5 | **Implemented** | Kick unchanged. Hat is the off-beat 8th. New synthesised 909-ish clap (three noise bursts 10 ms apart plus a filtered tail), once per bar on beat 2 — the book's sparser Dark Berlin variant, not the 2-and-4 default |
| 2 | Swing 50–54 %, hats and sparse perc only | **Implemented** | 52 % → `(15000/114.5)×(2/50)` = **5.24 ms**, applied to the off-beat hat and the ghost 16th. Kick and clap are dead straight |
| 3 | Everything except the kick into the delay | **Implemented** | Stabs, pad, motif, hats, clap and the sparse element all feed the send. The kick does not — it stays the anchor |
| 4 | Dotted-8th ~391 ms at ~20 % feedback, plus a free tap ~264 ms at ~85 % | **Implemented** | Tap 1 = `beat × 0.75` (393.0 ms at 114.5 BPM, 391.3 at 115), feedback 20 %, crossfeed 55 % for ping-pong. Tap 2 = a fixed 264 ms, feedback 85 %, crossfeed 20 %, summed at 0.55. Both are Surge XT Effects `Delay` instances |
| 5 | Feedback band-limited 200 Hz–5 kHz, multiband saturation with the low band disabled, limiter last | **Partial** | Low-cut 200 Hz and high-cut 5 kHz set on both taps via Surge's own feedback EQ. **Multiband saturation with the low band disabled: skipped** — Surge XT Effects freezes its parameter names at load time so a second effect type cannot be addressed in the same chain, and pedalboard has no multiband saturator. The saturation that exists is on the kick+bass bus, i.e. the low band only — the opposite of what the book asks. The limiter is last but it sits on the mix, not inside the feedback loop |
| 6 | Chord high-passed ~80 Hz with resonance, −7 dB at 600 Hz, capped 4.5 kHz, short amp env, long filter-env attack | **Mostly** | New `patch_pad_hp`: HP 24 dB at 80 Hz, resonance 20 %, **noise oscillator on** ("give the delays something to grab onto"), amp env A 11 ms / D 350 ms / S 12 %. Post-EQ: peaking **−7 dB at 600 Hz, Q 0.7**, low-pass 4.5 kHz. **Long filter-envelope attack skipped** — Surge routes the filter EG through its modulation matrix, which pedalboard does not expose as named parameters |
| 7 | Kick bell-cut ~4.5 dB at the bass fundamental; first 16th of each beat empty in the bass | **Implemented** | Peaking **−4.5 dB, Q 1.4, at `root_hz`** (55.0 Hz for a-11) on the kick stem. Bass gated to silence for the first 16th of every beat with 3 ms ramps; sidechain duck depth cut from 0.72 to 0.35 because the gate now does that work |
| 8 | Sparse elements on bar periods coprime with the loop (3 vs 4) | **Partial — and the conflict is structural** | A period only survives a butt-splice if it *divides* the clip. Round 1 was 29 bars — prime — so nothing but a 1-bar period tiled it. Clip length is now rounded to the nearest multiple of 6 bars (**30 bars, 62.9 s**, rather than 29 bars / 60.8 s), and the sparse horn runs on a **3-bar period** against the listener's implied 4. True coprimality — a period that never realigns — is incompatible with a seamless single-clip loop **by construction**; it belongs to the layering engine that concatenates clips, where 3 clips against 4 does exactly what the book describes |
| 9 | Three modulators at ~1 Hz / 0.25 Hz / ≥ 2 min, separate phases | **Partial** | ~1 Hz on the source (bed) filter and 0.25 Hz on the send filter, each quantised to a whole number of cycles per clip so the loop stays seamless (**1.003 Hz** and **0.247 Hz** at 62.9 s), on phases drawn from the seed. The **≥ 2-minute drift is capped at one cycle per clip** — a modulator slower than the loop cannot exist inside the loop. Same answer as rule 8: it belongs one level up |
| 10 | LRA ≤ 4 LU, ≤ 400 onsets/min, mono below 120 Hz, no tempo or key change | **Implemented** | LRA **0.7–0.8 LU**; onsets **281–335/min**; `mono_below(120 Hz)` applied per bus — it is linear, so per-bus and per-mix give the same result and the stems still sum exactly; one tempo and one key throughout, as before |

## What got worse, and why

**Slow timbral drift halved: 0.22–0.32 → 0.14–0.17** (references 0.41–0.46). This
is a direct cost of rules 3 and 6. With the sends near 100 % the delay return is
most of the mix, and a delay return is spectrally far more stable than the dry
signal feeding it; meanwhile the chord's short envelope removes the sustained
material that the source-filter LFO used to sweep. Modulating the return instead
of the dry signal recovered some of it but not most. The book's own answer is the
one it gives in rule 9 — a modulator measured in minutes — which a 63-second loop
cannot contain. **This metric will not be fixed inside a single clip.**

**2–8 kHz rose from 0.11–0.18 % to 0.50–0.55 %**, marginally over the ≤0.5 %
guidance and just above ref01's 0.44 %. The clap is the whole of it; it was
already capped with a double low-pass at 2.6 kHz and its level cut to 0.42.

**Rule 6 could not be taken fully literally.** A 0.75-beat chord note — the honest
reading of "short amp envelope, the echoes fill it out" — collapsed the body region
to 39.5 %, well under the floor. Notes are 1.5 beats with the send raised to ~100 %.
The echoes do supply the sustain, but the dry note could not be as short as Diva's
in the source article.

**Two solver bounds had to be relaxed for the book variants.** With the chord's
shape now fixed by rules 6 and 5, its *level* no longer carries the "sits behind"
intent, so the variant ceiling of 1.25× was starving the body region — v1 sat at
39.6 % with 35 % below 60 Hz until the chord and fx bounds were widened. For the
same reason the pattern-book path floors a variant's pad cut at 0.75: the book's
high-pass, scoop and short envelope already put the chord behind the sub, and
stacking a level cut on top double-thins it.

**Clip length changed from ~60.8 s to ~62.9 s** to make rule 8's 3-bar period tile
the loop (30 bars instead of a prime 29). All the measurements above are rates and
shares, so they stay comparable.
