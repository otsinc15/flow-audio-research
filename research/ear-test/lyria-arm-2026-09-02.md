# Lyria RealTime arm — 8 × 90 s, 2026-09-02

Google's **Lyria RealTime** (`models/lyria-realtime-exp`) rendered through a new
harness in `lyria/`, measured on the same pipeline as every other arm
(`measure-clips.py`, librosa 0.11, ffmpeg EBU R128).

Audio: `~/Documents/AI Agent Outputs/Artifacts/flow-audio-candidates/round5-lyria/`
(wav + m4a + `manifest.json`) — **uncommitted, as always.**
Raw metrics: `out/lyria-measurements.json` (also uncommitted).

**Bottom line up front.** The API works, the controls are real, and live steering
is genuinely audible — but the raw audio is the most spectrally broken of any arm
so far: **87–97 % of all energy sits below 150 Hz and the 150–500 Hz body region
is 2.8–6.9 %** against a 15–45 % target. One clip ships **over 0 dBFS**. This is
a promising *instrument* attached to a mix that would need heavy corrective EQ
before anyone could listen to it for an hour.

---

## What was run

Identical five weighted prompts on all eight clips:

> `hypnotic minimal dub techno:1.0, deep sub bass:0.8, dry analog kick:0.6, dubby chord stabs with delay:0.6, sparse percussion:0.4`

with `scale = C_MAJOR_A_MINOR` (the A-minor enum — the model does not distinguish
relative keys), `guidance = 4.0`, and `music_generation_config` otherwise as per
the table below. Clips 04 and 08 were steered live, raising `brightness` in four
steps (0.35 / 0.40 / 0.48 / 0.55) at 15 / 30 / 45 / 60 s with everything else held.

One line reproduces the whole batch:

```sh
~/.venvs/lyria/bin/python lyria/render.py --batch lyria/batch.json --out-dir out
```

**Transport was clean on the run that counted**: all 8 clips hit exactly 90.0 s,
45 chunks each, **zero reconnects**, no safety-filtered prompts, max inter-chunk
gap 2.5–3.3 s. Output on the wire was `audio/l16;rate=48000;channels=2`, i.e. the
documented 48 kHz 16-bit stereo, confirmed rather than assumed.

## What works, with evidence

**1. `bpm` is accurate and rock-solid.** 118 → 117.5 measured on all four clips;
122 → 123.0 on all four. `beat_interval_cv` 0.006–0.025, i.e. a machine-tight
grid. Both settings land inside the 114–122 reference window. This is the single
most reliable control in the API.

**2. Live steering demonstrably works — this is the real finding.** The brightness
sweep is not subtle and it is not imagined. Comparing each steered clip against
its fixed-brightness twin, per-10 s 95 % spectral rolloff:

| 10 s segment | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 06 `t122-d40-b40` (fixed) | 7836 | 8533 | 8688 | 8306 | 8632 | 7803 | 9016 | 8995 | 9561 |
| **08 `t122-d40-bsweep` (steered)** | 7004 | 7173 | 7615 | 8023 | **9504** | **11629** | **13157** | **14246** | 13989 |

The steered clip **doubles its rolloff, 7.0 → 14.2 kHz**, and the rise begins in
segment 5 — exactly where the 45 s and 60 s steer steps land — while the control
clip stays flat within its own noise. Clip 04 shows the same shape
(10357 → 13569 Hz). Mid-stream parameter changes reach the audio within a couple
of seconds, as documented, and no reconnect or dropout was needed to apply them.

**3. `brightness` maps monotonically onto measured centroid** at both tempos
(118: 1978 → 2118 → 3946 Hz for 0.3/0.4/0.5; 122: 2254 → 2172 → 3198). The jump
from 0.4 to 0.5 is much larger than 0.3 to 0.4 — the dial is not linear.

**4. Energy is extremely constant.** LRA 0.9–1.6 LU against a ≤ 4 LU target, and
`rms_per_10s_range` 0.8–2.4 dB. Nothing ever "happens", which for functional audio
is the goal. (It is arguably *too* flat — the references sit at 4.1 LU, so Lyria
is more frozen than the thing we are trying to match, the same failure the
ElevenLabs arm had.)

## Defects

**1. The spectrum is broken. This is the headline.** Every clip puts **87–97 % of
its energy below 150 Hz** (target 40–55 %) and **2.8–6.9 % in the 150–500 Hz
body region** (target 15–45 %; ref01 is at 46 %). Between 36 % and 74 % of total
energy is under 60 Hz alone, where ref01 sits at 27.6 % and ref02 at nearly zero.

I checked whether this was an artefact before believing it: it is not DC offset
(mean sample value +0.002 to +0.005) and it is not subsonic rumble (< 20 Hz
carries 0.13–0.54 %). The energy genuinely lives in the 20–150 Hz octave. The
prompt asked for "deep sub bass" at weight 0.8 and "dubby chord stabs" at 0.6,
and the model appears to have honoured the first while all but ignoring the
second. On earbuds or a laptop speaker — which reproduce almost nothing below
100 Hz — most of these clips would be close to inaudible.

**2. Clip 03 clips.** `t118-d50-b50` measures **+0.4 dBFS true peak** (sample peak
+0.07). That is raw model output over full scale, with no mastering applied by us.
Anything shipping this needs a limiter, and the harness cannot assume headroom.

**3. Level is inconsistent and runs quiet.** Integrated loudness spans **−15.8 to
−17.9 LUFS across clips with identical guidance and prompts** — a 2.1 LU spread
from nothing but generation variance. Six of eight fall below the −16 LUFS floor.
Per-clip normalisation would be mandatory in any product.

**4. `density` is directional at best.** At 118 BPM onsets/min rises cleanly with
density (299 → 421 → 475). At 122 BPM it does not (483 → 365 → 487). Five of
eight clips also exceed the ≤ 400 onsets/min target, including the two nominally
"sparse" 0.3-density clips. The dial does something, but it is not dependable
enough to build a "calm ↔ busy" product axis on without measuring every render.

**5. The timbre is frozen.** Slow centroid CV is **0.08–0.15** against a
0.41–0.46 reference band. Only the steered clip 08 reaches 0.25, and only because
*we* moved the dial. Left alone, Lyria holds a colour and does not drift — the
gentle unforced movement that both references show is absent, and on this evidence
it has to be scripted in from outside rather than expected from the model.

**6. Transport is flaky enough to matter.** The first attempt at this batch died:
a session dropped at exactly 30.0 s with `APIError: 1006 abnormal closure
[internal]`. That failure is transient (the re-run was perfect, 8/8, zero
reconnects), but it is frequent enough that reconnect handling is load-bearing.
A reconnect restarts the model's musical context, so it leaves an audible seam —
the harness reports `reconnects` per clip so a seamed render can be discarded
rather than trusted. Throughput is also **slower than realtime**: ~92 s of wall
clock per 90 s clip, which rules out generating on-device in front of a listener
at 1× without buffering.

## Listening notes — read the caveat

**I did not listen to these clips.** I have no audio playback in this environment,
and I am not going to dress measurements up as ears. Everything above is
instrument-derived. What the numbers license me to say, and no more:

- The 118 BPM clips will read as a *credible tempo and a genuinely steady grid*.
  Nothing in the data suggests wobble, drift or a wandering pulse.
- Clips 01, 02, 05, 06 and 08 will sound **bass-heavy to the point of muddy**, and
  on small speakers, thin-to-empty — a sub and a kick with very little between
  them. 96.6 % below 150 Hz (clip 05) is not a mix, it is a bass part.
- Clip 03 is the brightest of the fixed clips (centroid 3946 Hz, 1.09 % above
  8 kHz) *and* the one that clips. Expect it to sound both the most complete and
  the most likely to spit on transients.
- The sweep in 04 and 08 should be plainly audible as an opening-up over the first
  minute. Whether it reads as *musical* or as *someone turning a filter knob* is
  exactly the question the measurement cannot answer, and it is the main thing
  worth Daniel's ears.

**The one question to answer by ear:** does the steering feel like music breathing,
or like automation? If it feels like music, the mix problems are fixable with EQ
and a limiter and this arm is live. If it feels mechanical, no amount of EQ saves it.

## Terms

From Google's Gemini API [Additional Terms of Service](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms),
on ownership of generated output:

> "Google won't claim ownership over that content. You acknowledge that Google may
> generate the same or similar content for others and that we reserve all rights
> to do so."

and on responsibility:

> "You're responsible for your use of generated content, and for the use of that
> content by anyone you share it with."

**No explicit commercial-use grant or prohibition for music output was found** —
not on either Lyria documentation page, and not in those terms. They neither
authorise nor forbid commercial use in so many words, and they impose no
production restriction on Experimental models beyond "The Services include
experimental technology and may sometimes provide inaccurate or offensive
content." Two things that *are* stated and do matter:

- **Every output is watermarked.** "Output audio is always watermarked for
  identification following our Responsible AI principles" (SynthID). There is no
  opt-out.
- **Data handling depends on billing.** Unpaid: "Google uses the content you
  submit to the Services and any generated responses to provide, improve, and
  develop Google products and services and machine learning technologies." Paid:
  "Google doesn't use your prompts ... or responses to improve our products."

The "same or similar content for others" clause is the one with teeth for a
catalogue business: it explicitly reserves Google's right to hand a competitor
something that sounds like your library. Commercial licensing stays an **open
question requiring Google's own confirmation** before anything built on this ships.

## Verdict

Lyria RealTime is the first arm whose **controls behave like an instrument** —
accurate tempo, monotonic brightness, and live steering that measurably lands in
the audio within seconds. That is a real capability the Surge/code arm has to
build by hand and the ElevenLabs arm cannot do at all.

But the raw output is the **worst-mixed** of any arm: no midrange, a sub that
swamps everything, one clip over 0 dBFS, and a 2 LU level spread between
identically-configured renders. It is a synthesiser with a broken output stage.
Whether that is fatal depends entirely on whether the *musical* material underneath
is good — corrective EQ can rebalance a spectrum, but it cannot invent a chord part
that the model declined to play.

Against the venture's own moat test — felt effect, growing catalogue, honest money
— this arm currently supplies **steerability** and fails **quality**, and the
licensing question is unresolved rather than settled. It should not be scored
until Daniel has listened to 04 and 08.
