# Competitor Card: Focus@Will (now evolving into focus.music)

## Identity

- Science-based focus music streaming service. Curated "channels" of pre-recorded, remixed instrumental music, chosen via a brain-type quiz. Targets ADHD and productivity users hard.
- Launched 2012 (web beta ~2011, iOS app May 2013). Based in Los Angeles / Marina Del Rey, CA.
- Company: Focus@Will Labs, Inc. App Store seller: Dulcetta Inc (the original dev company, same team). Independent, no evidence of acquisition.
- Platforms: iOS, Android, Mac, web player.
- Pricing: free trial, then ~$7.49–9.99/month or ~$52.49–52.99/year. Workplace/team plans ~$22–27 per seat/year. Lifetime deals ($70–300) sold via third-party deal sites, not first-party.
- KEY MOVE: In 2026 the company launched a new app, **focus.music** (iOS, April 2026), pitched as "the same science… rebuilt to adapt to your brain in real time." Existing Focus@Will accounts carry over. The old app is in maintenance mode; focus.music is the active product.

## What users praise

- The music genuinely works for focus, especially ADHD brains — this is a real, loyal niche. Quiz-matched channels ("brain type") are a differentiator people remember.
- "The first time I downloaded this app and started my first session, three hours went by without my notice." (4★, May 2026)
- "This app was recommended by a doctor after I was diagnosed with ADHD… the stations it recommended put my brain in the focus and 'get stuff done' mode." (5★, Sep 2022)
- "This app is way better than Endel or any other focus soundscapes app. The key is you don't even really hear or notice the music." (5★, Sep 2024)

## What users complain about

- The app is broken and unmaintained: "Something went wrong" errors, Bluetooth playback dead since iOS 18, reversed play/pause buttons, offline mode broken, Mac app won't play on M3.
- Support is gone: Zendesk says "The company you're looking for is no longer using our help center." Cancellation requests ignored; users still charged.
- Repetitive content: "thousands of hours" is really "multiple numbered remixes of the same few tracks" — habituation kills the effect after ~2 years.
- "No updates for 2 years; no support!! Lifetime member disappointed… the app appears to have been abandoned, even though its paywall is clearly still managed." (1★, Oct 2024)
- "Support not available, doesn't work with my Bose Bluetooth headphones… in app 'support' link opens to ZenDesk which says 'The company you're looking for is no longer using our help center.'" (1★, May 2026)
- "the 'thousands of hours' of music that F@W boasts actually consists of multiple numbered remixes of the same few tracks." (3★, Sep 2023)

## Recent moves 2024–2026

- 2024–2025: legacy iOS app left untouched (last update Aug 2022); complaint volume about broken playback, dead support, and forced-subscription onboarding grew. Trustpilot at 2.9/5.
- 2026-04-10: launched **focus.music** on iOS — adaptive "rebuilt to adapt to your brain in real time," Guided/Manual modes, ADHD-forward positioning. Existing subscribers migrate with no re-purchase.
- 2026: Android listing for old app now says "the latest Android version of Focus@Will is now Focus.Music… run by the same team." Active updates through Aug 2026 (v2026.8.95).
- Read: a quiet, self-funded reboot. No acquisition, no layoffs, no press. One small team trying to modernize a 14-year-old asset.

## Threat read

- **Copy the positioning, not the tech debt.** Focus@Will proves durable demand for ADHD-brain focus music and quiz-based personalization ("which channel fits your brain") — the venture should keep that onboarding hook. But their pre-recorded remixed library habituates after ~2 years; truly generative, high-variety audio is exactly the fix, and their own users name repetition as the reason they leave.
- **Avoid their failure mode: maintenance and support.** 1★ reviews cluster on broken playback (Bluetooth, offline), dead support channels, and being charged with no recourse. A new entrant wins lapsed Focus@Will subscribers simply by having an app that plays over Bluetooth and a human who answers. Reliability is the moat they gave away.
- **The opening is now.** Their legacy app is abandoned and their successor (focus.music) is 5 months old with 4 US ratings and no marketing volume (brand demand ~680 searches/mo US). They have the science story but no generative capability, no visual layer, and burned goodwill with their most loyal (lifetime, ADHD) users. A polished generative-audio app with a calm interactive visual layer can position directly against their ashes: "focus music that never repeats, from a team that ships."
