# What users like and dislike, and where the real moat is (2026-09-02)

Synthesis of two primary-source passes run today. Every number is traceable to raw files:
- `research/review-mining/cross-competitor-likes-dislikes.md` — 3,047 iTunes reviews, US + DE
  storefronts, 8 apps (Endel, Brain.fm, Focus@Will, myNoise, Noisli, Portal, Mubert, Dark Noise).
  iOS/macOS only; no Google Play.
- `research/moat-research/summary-2026-09-02.md` — 22 Perplexity sonar-pro queries over Reddit, forums,
  press and company pages, every claim tagged first-party / user post / third-party / unknown. Quotes are
  paraphrase-grade until the threads are opened.
- Corrections in `research/competitor-sweep/endel/card.md` (Endel is stem assembly, not generative) and
  Daniel's first-hand note that the May 2026 Deeper Focus re-release changed only the control, not the sounds.

## What people like (why they stay)

1. **It works, and they can feel it.** Of 1,860 positive reviews, 32% credit an effect on focus, 19% calm,
   19% sleep. Among reviewers who describe a durable habit ("for years", "every day", 230 reviews), a felt
   focus effect is the thing they would miss most: Endel 53%, Brain.fm 56%, Focus@Will 81%.
2. **A catalogue that keeps growing.** Variety is mentioned by only 5.6% of positives, but it is the code
   with the highest lift among long-term users (1.93×). myNoise, the lowest-negative paid app (25%), adds
   generators "almost every week" and states 3,000+ recordings (first-party).
3. **Being treated fairly on money.** One-time purchase / no subscription (1.74× lift) and honesty (1.62×)
   are over-represented among long-term users.
4. **Sound quality** (1.64×) and **the same recognisable character every session.** One long-term Endel
   user: "I actually love that the sounds are quite repetitive… my brain immediately understands that it's
   time to work" (r/ProductivityApps, 2025-01-26). Repetition of *character* is a conditioning cue.

## What people dislike (why they leave or 1★)

1. **Money-trust.** Billing is the #1 driver of 1★ across all eight apps (18% of 635 one-star reviews) and it
   is almost entirely Endel: 122 of Endel's 377 negatives, plus 43 for upselling paying subscribers.
2. **Reliability.** Bugs/crashes lead 1★ for the six smaller apps (27%); Focus@Will has 100 UX-bug negatives.
3. **Catalogue exhaustion.** Small in reviews (Endel 4.5% of all reviews) but present in the churn stories of
   all three paid focus products: Endel "7 days and you have heard it all"; Brain.fm cancelled on day 5,
   tracks "became quite tedious"; Focus@Will "the same 10–12 songs". Daniel's own churn reason.
4. **"It's free on YouTube."** Portal's #3 complaint; the substitute is always present.
5. **Abandonware.** Noisli's #2 complaint; small apps die of neglect, and users notice.

## What is NOT a moat

- **Design.** 20% of positives, but flat among long-term users (1.07×): it wins week one, not year two.
- **Science.** Brain.fm has a real 2024 paper; users cite it (1.71× lift in reviews) but not one forum
  user names it as the reason they pay. They argue from felt effect.
- **Variety as a selling point.** It does not appear in acquisition language anywhere. It retains.
- **Adaptivity / AI.** 1.3% of positives mention it.

## The real moat, as far as the evidence goes

Three things stacked, none of them a feature: (1) a sound that measurably puts the user in the state, and
that stays recognisable session to session; (2) a catalogue that grows faster than the user habituates,
which is what Endel has failed at for five years on Deeper Focus; (3) clean, honest money. Every incumbent
has at most two of the three. Endel: 1 and arguably not 2 or 3. Brain.fm: 1 and 3, weak 2. myNoise: 2 and 3,
weaker 1 for focus. Nobody in the corpus has all three.

Caveats: App Store reviewers are self-selected; no cancellation survey exists anywhere for this category;
forum quotes are Perplexity paraphrases; Android is absent.
